Why bundling beats a generic "you may also like" row

A recommendation row asks the shopper to start a new browsing session. A frequently bought together block asks a much smaller question: do you want the thing that completes this? One is a detour, the other is a one-click completion of the purchase already in progress — which is why complement-based cross-sell consistently outperforms generic similarity rows.

The quality of the pairing is everything. A phone case next to a phone is a completion. Another phone next to a phone is a competitor to your own sale, and it slows the decision down. The practical rule: cross-sell complements, never substitutes.

Bundles, add-ons and cart upsells are different jobs

Frequently bought together proposes complete products that pair with the current one. Product add-ons attach optional extras to a single product — gift wrap, engraving, a warranty — that are not standalone purchases. AI cart upsell evaluates the whole cart and picks one offer for it, rather than being anchored to one product.

Most stores run bundles on the product page and a cart-level offer behind it, then use funnel analytics to check the two are not competing for the same click. If a pairing needs a discount to move, a tiered reward or a Buy X Get Y rule is usually a cheaper lever than cutting the bundle price outright.

Frequently asked questions

What does "frequently bought together" mean on Shopify?

It is a block that shows products commonly purchased alongside the one a shopper is viewing, with a single action that adds the whole set to the cart. It usually sits on the product page below the add-to-cart button, in the cart, or both.

Does Shopify have frequently bought together built in?

Shopify has a native product-recommendations endpoint that powers "you may also like" style rows in many themes, but it does not give you a one-click multi-product bundle block with its own analytics, manual pairing overrides, or A/B testing. That is what an app adds.

How does NUO AI decide which products to show?

It matches companion products using catalog relationships and order signals, and you can override any pairing manually when you know a better match. Each pairing's views, add rate, and attributed revenue are reported, so the matching is auditable rather than a black box.

Should I offer a discount on the bundle?

Not by default. Test the bundle at full price first — if the pairing is genuinely complementary, a meaningful share of shoppers will take it without a discount, and you keep the margin. Add an incentive only when the un-discounted add rate proves too low.

Will a bundle block slow my product page down?

It should not. NUO AI's widgets inherit your theme's existing typography and button styles and render inline rather than pulling in a separate design framework, which keeps layout shift near the add-to-cart button to a minimum.

Can I run frequently bought together and a cart upsell at the same time?

Yes, and most stores do — a product-page bundle plus one cart-level offer. Check the funnel report afterwards to confirm the two offers are not cannibalising the same click rather than adding incremental revenue.
