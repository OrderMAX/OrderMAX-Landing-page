How to stack discounts on Shopify without losing margin

Shopify can combine discounts — but only in the ways you explicitly permit, and the defaults are not a strategy. Stacking is either a deliberate ladder you designed or an accident that shows up in next month's margin report. Here is how the mechanism works and how to keep it on the first side of that line.

How Shopify discount combinations work

Shopify sorts discounts into classes — product discounts, order discounts, and shipping discounts. When you create a discount you choose which other classes it is allowed to combine with. Those combination settings, not the shopper, decide what stacks.

  • An automatic discount and a discount code can apply to the same order when both are marked as combinable with each other's class.
  • Two discounts in the same class generally do not both apply — a shopper cannot usually compound two order-level codes.
  • A shipping discount combining with a product discount is the most common and safest stack: different classes, predictable cost.
  • If you leave combinations off, the discount applies alone and Shopify picks a single applicable discount.

Because the behaviour depends on per-discount settings, the fastest way to know what your store currently does is to review the combinations toggles on your active discounts — not to reason about it from first principles.

Where stacking costs you money

Markdown plus code. A product already at 30% off, plus a 15% sitewide code, is 40.5% off — and if your gross margin was 45%, almost all of it is gone. Exclude sale items from codes, or cap codes to full-price collections.

Free shipping plus a percentage code. The percentage reduces revenue while shipping cost stays fixed, so the blended margin falls faster than the headline discount implies. Set a minimum spend on free shipping and keep it there.

Tier plus loyalty. A spend-threshold discount and a standing loyalty discount both landing on your best customers — see tiered discounts vs. loyalty programs. Give the two systems different reward currencies.

BOGO plus a code. Buy one get one free is already an effective 50% on two units. Anything on top of it is almost certainly below cost. Mark BOGO campaigns as non-combinable by default.

Gift plus everything. A free gift is cheap because it costs you the gift's cost, not a percentage. That makes it the safest thing to allow on top of other discounts — which is exactly why GWP is the better stacking partner than a second percentage.

A workable stacking policy

  1. Pick one percentage, maximum. Never allow two percentage discounts on the same order. One percentage plus one non-percentage reward is the pattern that stays solvent.
  2. Make non-percentage rewards your stacking layer. Free shipping, a free gift, or an add-on at no cost are all fixed-cost rewards, so the worst case is knowable in advance.
  3. Exclude sale items from codes. One rule that prevents the most common loss.
  4. Set a floor, and test against it. Decide your minimum acceptable blended margin, then place a real test order claiming every discount a shopper could legitimately claim at once. If the total is below the floor, a combination setting is wrong.
  5. Review after every campaign. Stacking damage accumulates through old discounts nobody turned off. Audit active discounts monthly.

Frequently asked questions

Can you stack discount codes on Shopify?

Only where you allow it. Shopify groups discounts into product, order and shipping classes, and each discount has combination settings controlling which other classes it can apply alongside. Two discounts in the same class generally will not both apply, while cross-class combinations such as a product discount plus a shipping discount can.

Can an automatic discount and a discount code apply together?

Yes, when both are configured as combinable with the other's discount class. If the combinations are not enabled, Shopify applies a single discount rather than compounding them.

How do I stop discounts from stacking?

Turn off the combination settings on each discount so it cannot combine with other classes, exclude sale and already-discounted items from your codes, and mark high-cost promotions such as BOGO as non-combinable by default.

Do stacked discounts multiply or add?

In practice they compound rather than add: a 30% markdown followed by a 15% code is roughly 40.5% off, not 45%. The total is still usually far more than you intended, which is why a floor and a test order matter more than the arithmetic.

Is stacking discounts ever a good idea?

Yes, when the second reward has a fixed cost. One percentage discount plus free shipping or a free gift makes the worst case knowable in advance, which is not true of two percentages. The safe pattern is one percentage discount, maximum, plus fixed-cost rewards on top.

How do I check what my store currently allows?

Review the combinations settings on each active discount in the Shopify admin, then place a real test order that claims every discount a shopper could legitimately claim at once and compare the final total against your margin floor.
