Gift with purchase

17 free gift with purchase ideas for Shopify stores

A free gift only raises order value if the shopper wants it and has to spend more to get it. Both halves matter. Here are 17 offer structures that work on Shopify stores, grouped by what you sell, plus the threshold arithmetic that decides whether the gift pays for itself.

First, the two rules every free gift offer obeys

Rule one: the gift must be wanted. A gift the shopper does not value is not an incentive, it is clutter in the cart. The strongest gifts are things the shopper would otherwise have bought, or a trial of something they will buy next.

Rule two: the threshold must require a stretch. If your average order is $64 and the gift unlocks at $50, you are handing out free product on orders that were already happening. The threshold has to sit above your current average order value — typically 15–30% above — so the gift buys incremental spend rather than subsidising normal spend.

Everything below assumes both rules. Get them wrong and the best gift idea in the world still loses money.

Ideas by category

Beauty, skincare and fragrance

  • Deluxe sample of the next size up. The gift is a trial of a product with a higher price point, which seeds the following order.
  • Travel-size version of the cart's hero product. Reinforces the purchase rather than distracting from it.
  • Applicator or tool. A brush, sponge or spatula — low cost, high perceived usefulness, and it increases consumption of what they bought.
  • Sample trio with a "pick one" choice. Choice lifts acceptance and tells you which product has pull. See pick your gift.

Apparel and accessories

  • Matching small accessory. Socks with shoes, a scrunchie with a dress — completes the look at trivial cost.
  • Reusable tote or dust bag. Doubles as branded impressions every time it is used.
  • Garment care kit. Suede spray, a lint brush, a repair patch. Reduces returns as a side effect.

Food, drink and supplements

  • Single-serve of a new flavour. The cheapest possible product launch test.
  • Shaker, scoop or branded glass. Durable, used daily, stays in the kitchen.
  • Recipe or protocol card. Near-zero cost, raises consumption, drives the repeat order.

Electronics, home and hardware

  • Cable, adapter or mount. The accessory they would have bought separately within a week.
  • Spare consumable. An extra filter, blade or battery.
  • Protective case or cover. High perceived value, modest cost, fewer damage claims.

Any store

  • Free shipping as the "gift". Still a threshold reward; often the cheapest one you have.
  • Mystery gift. Works for clearing slow stock, but set expectations honestly — a disappointing mystery gift costs trust.
  • Gift ladder. A sample at $50, a full-size at $120. This is a tiered reward, and it outperforms a single threshold on most catalogs.
  • Gift on a specific product, not on spend. Use this to push a hero SKU or move a slow companion item.

The threshold maths, in one worked example

Say your AOV is $70. Your gift costs you $9 and retails at $29. You set the threshold at $90.

  • A shopper at $70 adds a $25 item to qualify. You gained $25 of revenue and gave up $9 of cost — clearly positive, assuming normal gross margin on that $25.
  • A shopper already at $95 claims the gift without changing behaviour. That one costs you $9 for nothing. This is the leak.
  • So the offer is profitable when incremental orders outnumber the free-riders by enough to cover their cost — which is a measurable thing, not a guess.

This is exactly why the number to watch is not acceptance rate but discount cost against the order values that claimed it. NUO AI's funnel analytics report both in the same view, so a leaking threshold is visible within days instead of at the end of the quarter.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good free gift to offer on Shopify?

Something the shopper already wants, that costs you little, and that increases the use of what they bought — a deluxe sample, a matching accessory, a consumable spare, or a tool. Avoid gifts that are unrelated to the purchase: they read as inventory you could not sell.

How much should a free gift cost me?

A common working range is 5–15% of the threshold value. On a $90 threshold that means a gift costing you roughly $5–13. Price it against your cost, not its retail price — the shopper values the retail price, you pay the cost, and that gap is what makes GWP cheaper than an equivalent discount.

Where should I set the gift threshold?

Roughly 15–30% above your current average order value. Below AOV and you fund gifts on orders that were already going to happen; far above it and too few shoppers can reach it to matter.

Do free gifts actually increase average order value?

Giving shoppers a visible, concrete reason to cross a spend threshold is a well-established AOV lever in ecommerce. Whether it is profitable for your store depends entirely on the threshold and the gift's cost, which is why the discount cost per claimed order is the number to track.

Can I offer a choice of free gifts?

Yes. Letting the shopper choose from two to four approved options typically lifts acceptance because the gift stops being random, and the pick rate tells you which product has real pull with your buyers.

How long should a gift with purchase campaign run?

Long enough to produce a readable sample — usually two to four weeks for most stores — then either keep it as an always-on threshold or rotate the gift. Permanently running the same gift tends to erode its perceived value.
